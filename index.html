1000 BCE

Era Ic. 1000 BCE – 609 CE

The Age of Incense & Verse

عصر ما قبل الإسلام · الجاهلية

Long before empires rose, the peninsula was an ocean of sand crossed by camel caravans. Frankincense and myrrh from the south flowed north to Petra, Gaza and Rome, while in the seasonal markets of the Hejaz, poets competed for glory with words said to be sharper than swords.

Trade

The Frankincense Road

Resins harvested in Dhofar and Hadramawt travelled more than 2,000 km by caravan to the Mediterranean. Oases along the route grew wealthy by offering water, protection and markets.

Kingdoms

Saba, Kindah & the Nabataeans

The Sabaeans engineered the great dam of Ma'rib; the Kindah ruled from Qaryat al-Faw; and the Nabataeans carved monumental tombs at Hegra and Petra.

Poetry

The Suspended Odes

Oral poetry was the archive of Arabian life. By tradition, the finest poems — the Mu'allaqāt — were honoured by being hung upon the Kaaba in Mecca.

“Halt, my two friends, and let us weep for the memory of a beloved and an abode…”— opening of the Mu'allaqa of Imru' al-Qays

The Light from Mecca

فجر الإسلام والخلافة الراشدة

In 610 CE, in the Cave of Hira above Mecca, the revelation of the Qur'an began. Within a single generation, a peninsula of rival tribes was united by one faith — and from Medina, the message spread across three continents.

Arches of the Golden Age

الدولتان الأموية والعباسية

As the caliphate's capital moved to Damascus and then Baghdad, the peninsula became the spiritual heart of a civilisation stretching from al-Andalus to Central Asia. Pilgrim roads converged on Mecca and Medina, while in great cities scholars, architects and physicians lit a golden age of learning.

Era IV1727 – 1891

Walls of Mud, Roots of a Nation

الدولة السعودية الأولى والثانية

On the banks of Wadi Hanifa, in the oasis of Diriyah, a new state was born in 1727. From the sun-baked mud-brick palaces of at-Turaif, the House of Saud brought much of the peninsula under one rule — fell — and rose again.

At-Turaif District

The citadel of Diriyah — a maze of adobe palaces, mosques and homes — was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2010.

Najdi Architecture

Thick mud-brick walls kept interiors cool; triangular vents and stepped crenellations crowned buildings, while palm trunks and fronds formed the ceilings.

Founding Day · 22 February

Since 2022, Saudi Arabia marks Founding Day to commemorate Imam Muhammad bin Saud's establishment of the First Saudi State in 1727.

The Great Unification

ملحمة التوحيد

In the winter of 1902, a young Abdulaziz bin Abdulrahman Al Saud rode out of exile in Kuwait with a small band of companions. Over the next three decades he would forge a kingdom from rival regions — and in 1938, the desert gave up a secret that would change the world.
